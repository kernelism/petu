# Golden lesson: why don't action and reaction cancel?

- **Document:** `openstax-university-physics-volume-1.pdf` (OpenStax University Physics Vol. 1)
- **Page:** 230 (Example 5.10, Figure 5.20). Also uses p228 (third-law features), p229 (Example 5.10 data), p231 (Example 5.11).
- **Question type:** "why"
- **Question (as the student types it):** "If every push has an equal and opposite push back, why don't they all cancel? How does the professor's cart ever speed up?"

## The student's likely confusion

They hold two true facts (third-law pairs are equal and opposite; equal and opposite forces cancel) and combine them wrongly. They have not noticed that the two forces in a pair act on *different* bodies, and that "cancel" only means something once you have chosen which body or group of bodies you are asking about. The page's own wording ("System 1", "internal forces") reads as bookkeeping jargon rather than as the answer to this question.

## Carrying example

The page's own Example 5.10, unchanged: professor 65.0 kg, cart 12.0 kg, equipment 7.0 kg; she pushes backward on the floor with 150 N; friction and air resistance on the cart total 24.0 N. These values make the effect visible: the floor's 150 N is large, the opposing 24 N is small, and the hands-on-cart force (52.5 N, Example 5.11) is clearly *not* 150 N, which itself surprises students. Every box drawn around any part of the group gives the same 1.5 m/s², which is the cross-check the lesson ends on.

## The scene

**Things**
- A floor line running the full width of the scene, low on the board.
- Three chips standing on it, left to right: **professor 65 kg**, **cart 12 kg** with **equipment 7 kg** sitting on top of it.
- Force arrows, thickness proportional to size, each tinted by the body it *acts on*:
  - floor → professor, forward, 150 N (thickest);
  - professor → floor, backward, 150 N, drawn *below* the floor line, on the floor;
  - professor's hands → cart, forward, and cart → hands, backward (same size; shown as "?" until beat 6, then 52.5 N);
  - friction on the cart, backward, 24 N (thin).
- A small "pair" bracket joining each third-law pair (the two 150 N arrows; the two hand arrows).
- Dashed lassos (system boundaries): System 1 around all three chips; later System 2 around cart + equipment; finally a brief lasso around the professor alone.
- A running tally to the right of the chips: forward vs backward bars that become a net force, then ÷ mass, then an acceleration readout.
- One acceleration readout per lasso, joined by "=" connectors at the end.

**Relations**
- Pair brackets: "equal and opposite, on different bodies".
- Lasso → tally: only arrows that cross the lasso from outside feed the tally (leader lines from those arrows into the bars).
- Internal arrows fade and get a small "cancel inside" note; the arrow acting on the floor greys out with "acts on the floor, not on her".

**Layout (in words)**
The chips sit left of centre, low, because the floor is the thing everything pushes against; the floor line extends right so the tally can grow above its right end without touching the chips. The professor→floor arrow sits under the floor line, deliberately separated from the floor→professor arrow above it, so "different bodies" is visible as "different sides of the line". Lassos are drawn loosely and asymmetrically around the chips (not boxes), and the tally hangs off the right side of whichever lasso is active, connected by leader lines. Nothing is mirrored or grid-aligned: the System 2 lasso is a tighter, smaller loop nested inside the System 1 loop's right half.

**Connectors to the PDF**

- p228 "acting on different bodies or systems" → the two arrowheads (one on the floor, one on her).
- p229 Example 5.10 data ("Her mass is 65.0 kg", "150 N", "24.0 N") → the chips and arrows they label.

## Beats

**1. Put everything in.**
- Say: "Let's use the professor from Example 5.10. She's 65 kilograms, the cart's 12, the equipment's 7. I'll draw every force as an arrow, and the thicker the arrow, the bigger the force."
- Board: floor line; three chips with masses; friction arrow on the cart (24 N, thin).
- Slides: scroll to p229, highlight "Her mass is 65.0 kg" and "a backward force of 150 N", then p230 point at Figure 5.20.
- Connections: page numbers → chip labels.

**2. A pair lands on two different things.**
- Say: "She pushes back on the floor with 150 newtons. The floor pushes her forward with 150. Equal and opposite, yes. But look where each arrow lands: one on the floor, one on her."
- Board: the two 150 N arrows appear, the backward one under the floor line, the forward one on her; a pair bracket joins them, each tinted by the body it acts on.
- Slides: p228 highlight "acting on different bodies or systems", then "they do not cancel each other".
- Connections: p228 phrases → pair bracket.

**3. A second pair, size unknown.**
- Say: "Same thing between her hands and the cart. She pushes the cart forward, the cart pushes her back, just as hard. We don't know how hard yet, so I'll leave a question mark. We'll find it."
- Board: the two hand arrows between professor and cart, labelled "?", with their own pair bracket.
- Slides: stay on Figure 5.20, point at the hands.
- Connections: none new; the "?" is a promise picked up in beat 6.

**4. Cancelling depends on what you draw a line around.**
- Say: "Here's the move that answers your question. Forces only cancel if they push on the same thing. So first decide what thing you're asking about. They all speed up together, so draw a line around all three."
- Board: dashed System 1 lasso around all three chips. The hand pair is now fully inside: both arrows fade, with "cancel inside" beside them. The professor→floor arrow greys out: "acts on the floor, not on us". Bright arrows left crossing the lasso: floor's 150 N forward, friction 24 N back.
- Slides: p230 highlight "System 1" in the caption; p231 highlight "internal forces (between components of a system) cancel".
- Connections: Figure 5.20 "System 1" → lasso; p231 phrase → faded hand arrows.

**5. What's left speeds them up.**
- Say: "Nothing cancels the floor's push, because its partner is pushing on the floor, outside our line. So, 150 forward minus 24 back is 126 newtons. Divide by all 84 kilograms and you get 1.5 metres per second squared."
- Board: tally grows off the lasso: bar 150 forward, bar 24 back, net 126 N; then "÷ 84 kg" (65 + 12 + 7 written under the chips, summed), readout "1.5 m/s²" pinned to the System 1 lasso. Leader lines from the two bright arrows into their bars.
- Slides: p230 point at the Solution's net-force and acceleration lines.
- Connections: bright arrows → tally bars.

**6. Move the line and a hidden force appears.**
- Say: "Now shrink the line to just the cart and the equipment. Her hands' push is suddenly crossing the line, so now it counts. These 19 kilograms speed up at 1.5 too, so they need 28.5 newtons net. Friction takes 24, so her hands must push with 52.5 newtons. Not 150. That's the question mark."
- Board: System 2 lasso, tighter, nested in the right half of System 1. The forward hand arrow brightens; both "?" labels become 52.5 N (pair bracket still joins them). Tally: 19 kg × 1.5 = 28.5 N, + 24 N = 52.5 N.
- Slides: p231 Example 5.11, highlight "is an external force acting on System 2".
- Connections: Figure 5.20 "System 2" → small lasso; "?" → 52.5 N.

**7. Every line tells the same story.**
- Say: "Last check: draw the line around her alone. The floor pushes her forward 150, the cart pushes her back 52.5. That's 97.5 newtons on 65 kilograms: 1.5 again. Whatever you draw a line around, you get the same answer."
- Board: brief lasso around the professor; her tally 150 − 52.5 = 97.5, ÷ 65 = 1.5 m/s². The three readouts (System 1, System 2, professor) joined by "=" connectors.
- Slides: p231 highlight "some of it accelerates the professor".
- Connections: three readouts linked; the page's sentence links to the professor's readout.

## The aha

A third-law pair never cancels, because its two forces push on different things. Forces cancel only when they push on the same thing inside the line you've drawn. The floor's push on the professor has no partner inside the line, so it speeds the whole group up.

## Check question

A 400 kg horse pulls a 600 kg cart. The ground pushes the horse forward with 1,200 N, and friction on the cart's wheels is 200 N. (a) What is the acceleration? (b) How hard does the horse pull the cart, and the cart pull back on the horse?

**Answer:** (a) Draw the line around horse + cart: the horse-cart pull is inside and cancels. Net = 1,200 − 200 = 1,000 N on 1,000 kg, so a = 1.0 m/s². (b) Line around the cart alone: 600 × 1.0 = 600 N net, plus 200 N friction, so the pull is 800 N, in both directions. Cross-check on the horse: 1,200 − 800 = 400 N on 400 kg = 1.0 m/s².

## Why this is good

- Answers the exact misunderstanding (pairs vs. cancelling) and says the resolving idea in the student's own words ("cancel") before naming the textbook's term ("system").
- Uses the page's own worked example and numbers, so the lesson and the book reinforce each other.
- One scene that keeps changing: the same arrows fade, brighten and get relabelled as the boundary moves. Later beats transform earlier drawings rather than adding new panels.
- Relations are drawn: thickness is size; tint is the body acted on; pair brackets and lasso crossings are the logic, visible.
- Predict then reveal: the "?" left in beat 3 is resolved in beat 6, and the value (52.5, not 150) is itself a small surprise.
- Ends on a consistency check that shows the idea works whatever line you draw, not just a single computed number.
- The check question moves to a new situation (horse and cart) and needs the idea, not the arithmetic pattern.

## Appendix (hidden): arithmetic

- System 1 mass: 65.0 + 12.0 + 7.0 = 84.0 kg.
- Net external force on System 1: 150 − 24.0 = 126 N. a = 126 / 84 = 1.5 m/s² (matches the book's Example 5.10).
- System 2 (cart + equipment) mass: 12.0 + 7.0 = 19.0 kg. Net = 19.0 × 1.5 = 28.5 N. Hands' push = 28.5 + 24.0 = 52.5 N (matches Example 5.11).
- Professor alone: forward 150 (floor), backward 52.5 (cart's reaction). Net 97.5 N. 97.5 / 65.0 = 1.5 m/s². Consistent.
- Check question: (1,200 − 200) / (400 + 600) = 1.0 m/s². Cart: 600 × 1.0 + 200 = 800 N. Horse: (1,200 − 800) / 400 = 1.0 m/s².
- Simplifications: vertical forces (weights, normal forces) are omitted, as the book does ("no net force in the vertical direction"). Friction is all on the cart (System 2), as the book's Example 5.11 assumes.
